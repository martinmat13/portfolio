## Motivation

Ethohydraulics examines the ways living organisms interact with fluid environments, which is crucial for understanding how aquatic fauna navigate turbulent river currents[cite: 1]. This project investigates the precise influence of vortex structures on fish performance by comparing two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) numerical models[cite: 1]. The primary goal is to evaluate the impact of model dimensionality on the accuracy of representing hydromechanical characteristics, which directly informs the design of bio-mimetic underwater vehicles[cite: 1].

## Methodology

*   **Domain & Geometry:** The computational domain measured 2.4m x 1.1m x 0.36m, utilizing a NACA 0025 airfoil with a 0.3m chord length to represent the fish-like body[cite: 1]. 
*   **Meshing:** The `blockMesh` utility generated the base structured mesh, while `snappyHexMesh` was executed to create high-quality, unstructured hexahedral and tetrahedral cells for refinement around the curving surfaces of the airfoil[cite: 1].
*   **Solver Setup:** Simulations were executed using `pimpleFoam`, a pressure-based solver built for transient, incompressible flows[cite: 1]. 
*   **Turbulence Modeling:** The $k-\omega$ SST (Shear Stress Transport) model was selected for its robust near-wall treatment, allowing correct resolution of the viscous sub-layer without requiring an excessively fine grid[cite: 1].
*   **Boundary Conditions:** A fixed inlet velocity of 1 m/s was established, alongside a no-slip condition applied directly to the fish surface[cite: 1].

## Results & Analysis

The comparative analysis across discrete time intervals (4s, 7s, and 10s) revealed significant deviations between the 2D and 3D implementations[cite: 1]. 

*   **Vortex Formation:** Visual inspections in ParaView showed that vortices fully formed earlier in the 3D model[cite: 1]. This occurs because three-dimensional flows possess inherent complexity that amplifies turbulence and vorticity mixing, a feature the 2D model cannot adequately capture[cite: 1].
*   **Pressure Distribution:** Pressure distributions along the airfoil surface were not in close agreement[cite: 1]. The 2D simulation ignores spanwise variations and boundary layer interactions, leading to an oversimplified aerodynamic profile[cite: 1].
*   **Aerodynamic Drag:** The drag coefficient ($C_d$) was evaluated using the following relationship[cite: 1]:

$$
C_d = \frac{F_d}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 A}
$$

*   The 3D simulation captured a substantially higher maximum drag coefficient of 3.21, whereas the 2D simulation yielded a maximum of only 0.000086[cite: 1].
*   The 3D simulation accurately captured larger variations in drag forces because the inclusion of spanwise flow variations more closely represents the surface area and physical geometry of real-world objects[cite: 1].

Ultimately, while 2D simulations offer simplified baseline insights, they cannot replace 3D simulations in capturing the full complexity of ethohydraulic phenomena[cite: 1].
