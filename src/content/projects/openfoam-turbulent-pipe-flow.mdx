## Motivation

Fully developed turbulent pipe flow is a canonical benchmark for validating
turbulence closures before applying them to more complex internal-flow
geometries. This study compares a RANS $k$–$\omega$ SST model against a
wall-resolved LES, focusing on how each captures near-wall behaviour.

## Methodology

Mesh resolution was driven by the near-wall scaling requirement

$$
y^+ = \frac{u_\tau y}{\nu}
$$

where $u_\tau = \sqrt{\tau_w / \rho}$ is the friction velocity. For the LES
case the first cell centre was targeted at $y^+ \approx 1$ across the full
Reynolds number sweep, which set the radial cell count independently of the
axial resolution.

Boundary layer growth was tracked using a simple PID controller on the
inlet mass flow rate to hold the bulk Reynolds number constant across mesh
refinements:

$$
\dot{m}(t) = \dot{m}(t-1) + K_p e(t) + K_i \sum e(t) + K_d \big(e(t) - e(t-1)\big)
$$

with $e(t) = Re_{target} - Re_{measured}(t)$.

## Results

- RANS under-predicted the near-wall velocity gradient at low $Re$ but
  converged to within 4% of LES at higher $Re$.
- LES resolved the expected log-law region cleanly once $y^+ < 1$ was
  enforced at the first cell.
- Wall shear stress agreement between the two models improved
  substantially once mesh independence was confirmed on both sides.

The case setup, `blockMeshDict`, and post-processing scripts are in the
linked repository. Swap the turbulence model in `constant/turbulenceProperties`
to reproduce either branch of the comparison.
