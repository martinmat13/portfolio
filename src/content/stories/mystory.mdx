My name is Martin Mathew, and I have always been driven by a deep curiosity to explore. Born in the lush landscapes of Kerala and raised in the bustling capital city of New Delhi, I developed an adventurous spirit and a fascination with how things move. 

That fascination started with automobiles. By the time I was in the 8th grade, I had already set my sights on a career in automotive engineering. This drive eventually led me back to my roots in the picturesque mountains of Kerala, where I pursued my degree in Mechanical Engineering at Government Engineering College, Idukki. It was there, amidst the hills, that I worked on my first technical projects and discovered a spark for computational science—a field that would eventually reshape my entire trajectory.

After graduating in 2020, my journey took an unexpected but defining turn. I joined Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders Limited (MDL) in Mumbai as a Graduate Apprentice. MDL is a powerhouse that builds warships and submarines for the Indian Navy, and suddenly, I found myself collaborating alongside former naval officers on complex engineering tasks. 

Having the rare opportunity to regularly visit and walk the decks of actual warships was a profound experience. It instilled in me a deep sense of patriotism, but also a quiet reverence. Each time I stepped aboard a massive vessel, I would silently hope that it would stand as a symbol of peace and prosperity for humanity, rather than a tool for war.

My time at the shipyard was transformative. Working closely with seasoned professionals and naval officers gave me unique, hands-on insights into the marine industry. The sheer scale and complexity of maritime engineering captivated me. When my contract ended, I realized I had found my niche: I wanted to bridge my growing fascination with computational science and my newfound passion for the marine domain. 

I knew that to explore innovative solutions at the intersection of these two fields, I needed to push my academic boundaries. I looked toward Germany. 

By the grace of God, and with the incredible support of exceptional faculty members and admission committees, I was granted the opportunity to pursue my Master’s at Technische Universität Dresden. Today, having proudly graduated from the Master’s program in Computational Modeling and Simulation with a specialization in Computational Engineering, I am ready for whatever the next chapter holds.
